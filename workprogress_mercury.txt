THE CONSTRAINT THAT BREAKS THE OBVIOUS DESIGN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  A web page cannot be opened on a device that has NEVER loaded it. Local
  storage keeps a page working AFTER first load; it cannot put the page on a
  phone that has never seen it.

  So the real requirement is NOT "internet at the delivery". It is:
      INTERNET AT LEAST ONCE, EVER, ON THAT DEVICE.
  That is a much softer requirement and probably satisfiable (town trip, site
  office satellite, setup before travelling out).

  Consequence: if it IS third-party freight, the app cannot live on the driver's
  phone, so it must be the CUSTOMER's receiving clerk's phone. That is the open
  question that decides the whole build.

AGREED ARCHITECTURE (design only - approved by nobody yet)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The offline app does NOT pre-load deliveries. That design fails immediately:
  a DN created today cannot reach a phone that has been offline a week.

  Instead the QR labels from Stage 6 carry a self-contained SIGNED payload
  (serial + DN + PO + item). The phone learns about the delivery FROM THE BOX,
  not from our server.

      phone = dumb capture device. Scans, timestamps, queues locally.
      server = does ALL verification and reconciliation on sync.
                7 of 7 -> acknowledged
                6 of 7 -> flag the short
                unknown serial -> flag it

  The phone never has to be right about anything. That is what makes it robust.
  Same flow online and offline; online just syncs instantly and fires the alert.

THREE RISKS FLAGGED UP FRONT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. iOS WIPES OFFLINE STORAGE after ~7 days of non-use (Safari's cap on
     script-writable storage). A phone that captures a delivery then sits in a
     drawer at a remote site CAN LOSE THE ACKNOWLEDGEMENT BEFORE IT SYNCS.
     Home-screen install exempts it but has historically been buggy.
     THIS IS THE MOST LIKELY WAY THIS DESIGN LOSES DATA.
  2. Device clocks are unverifiable offline. Store acknowledged-at (device,
     untrusted) and synced-at (server, trusted) SEPARATELY and flag implausible
     gaps. Matters for an ISO shop.
  3. Mercury is blind between delivery and sync. Proposed a "delivered, not yet
     acknowledged" report - cheap, and the thing that actually protects Alex.

  Also unresolved: storing this properly wants a DELIVERY ACKNOWLEDGEMENT
  doctype (per-item condition, damage photos, dual timestamps, audit trail).
  Paul's standing rule was no custom doctypes; this one probably earns it, but
  it has NOT been agreed.
